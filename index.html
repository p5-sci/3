Managing changes in the environment

Types of changes

  • Biological changes
  • Physical changes
  • Chemical changes

Biological changes

These only take place in living things but they do not involve chemical reactions.

Characteristics of biological changes

  • They form new substances
  • They are irreversible
  • They only take place in living things

Examples of chemical changes (as listed in the notes)

  • Moulting
  • Metamorphosis
  • Gain and weight loss
  • Growth
  • Germination
  • Fertilization
  • Ripening of fruits
  • Fruiting
  • Shedding of leaves

Managing body changes

Examples of body changes

  • Growth of hair
  • Growth of toe / finger nails
  • Body odour
  • Menstruation
  • Weight gain and loss
  • Falling sick

How to manage the body changes
The body changes above are managed through promotion of personal hygiene, sanitation and also feeding on a balanced diet / proper feeding.

Guidance on how to manage body changes

  • Always promote personal hygiene in order to look smart, prevent injuries and skin diseases.
  • Always promote sanitation in order to prevent diarrheal diseases.
  • Always feed on a balanced diet in order to prevent / avoid deficiency diseases.
  • Avoid smoking and alcoholism in order to have good health of body organs such as brain, liver and the kidneys.

Effects of biological changes

  • They cause organisms to multiply
  • They lead to the formation of new cells in organisms
  • They cause maturity of organisms
  • They lead to increase or decrease in weight and size of organisms

Importance of biological changes

  • They enable organisms to increase in number (reproduction)
  • They enable animals to increase in size through moulting and growth
  • Reproduction enables continuity of life
  • Ripening and fruiting enable organisms to get food

Chemical changes

These changes are irreversible and they involve chemical reactions.

Characteristics of chemical changes

  • They form new substances
  • They are irreversible
  • Substances change their mass, size, shape and weight.

Examples of chemical changes

  • Burning
  • Decomposition
  • Fermentation
  • Respiration
  • Photosynthesis
  • Rusting

Rusting

This is the formation of a brownish coating on iron when it reacts with oxygen and water.

Conditions necessary for rusting

  • Oxygen
  • Water
Iron + oxygen + water → rust

Examples of metals that do not rust

  • Aluminum
  • Brass
  • Gold
  • Stainless steel
  • Copper

Effects of rusting

  • It makes cutting tools blunt
  • It reduces the thickness of metals
  • It makes metals weak
  • It changes the color of metals
  • It makes screws and bolts hard to unscrew

Importance of rusting

  • It adds iron in soil hence improving soil's fertility

Methods used to prevent rusting

  • Lubrication: applying grease or oil on metals.
  • Painting
  • Galvanization: applying a coating of metal with a layer of zinc. Usually done on iron sheets.
  • Keeping metals in a dry place.

Similarities between rusting and burning

  • Both are chemical changes.
  • Both require oxygen to occur.

Differences between rusting and burning

  • Rusting requires both oxygen and water to occur while burning requires only oxygen.
  • Burning produces heat and light unlike rusting.
  • Rusting forms iron oxide while burning forms carbon.

Advantages (positive effects) of chemical changes

  • Burning produces heat which is used for cooking.
  • Respiration provides energy to the body.
  • Fermentation is used in brewing of alcohol.
  • Through decomposition, soil is formed.
  • Iron is added to soil through rusting.

Disadvantages (negative effects) of chemical changes

  • Rusting causes iron metals to wear out.
  • Chemical changes such as burning, fermentation, respiration and decomposition cause air pollution.
  • Changes such as decomposition, burning and rusting cause destruction of property.

Physical changes

These changes do not form new substances.

Characteristics of physical changes

  • Most of them are reversible.
  • No new substance is formed.
  • There is change in shape, size and form.
  • There is change in weight and volume.
  • The mass remains the same.

Examples of physical changes

  • Melting
  • Condensation
  • Shrinking
  • Dissolving of solutes in solvents
  • Expansion
  • Deposition
  • Freezing
  • Sublimation
  • Evaporation

Effects of physical changes in the environment

  • They lead to the formation of rain.
  • They lead to the formation of ice.
  • They lead to the formation of snow.
  • They lead to the formation of dew.
